Decarbonization Construction Technology

Electric Construction Report

Mapping the shift to electric equipment on construction sites across Canada.

Overview

Our latest research mapping the transition to electric construction sites across Canada — covering equipment readiness, grid capacity, cost modelling, and policy levers needed to accelerate adoption.

The construction industry accounts for a significant share of Canada's carbon emissions. As jurisdictions push for net-zero targets, the shift from diesel-powered to electric equipment on job sites is no longer optional — it's inevitable. This report provides the roadmap.

Working with industry leaders, equipment manufacturers, and policymakers, we analyzed the full landscape of electric construction technology: what's available today, what's coming, and what barriers remain.

Key findings

What we discovered.

01

Equipment Readiness

Over 60% of common construction equipment categories now have commercially available electric alternatives, with compact equipment leading adoption.

02

Grid Capacity

Urban construction sites can typically access sufficient grid power, but remote and large-scale sites face significant infrastructure gaps requiring portable charging solutions.

03

Cost Modelling

While upfront equipment costs remain 30–50% higher, total cost of ownership reaches parity within 3–5 years through reduced fuel, maintenance, and carbon pricing offsets.

04

Policy Levers

Procurement mandates and emissions-based site permits are proving more effective than subsidies alone in driving fleet electrification across jurisdictions.
